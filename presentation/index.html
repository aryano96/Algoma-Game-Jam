ThunderHacks II · Four Seasons

Perennial

One tree is shade in summer, food in fall and fire in winter.

Aryan · Algoma Game Jam 2026

Have the game open full screen with sound on, sitting on the title screen. Hook: “In Perennial, one tree is three things. In summer it’s shade, in fall it’s food, and in winter it’s firewood. You can’t have all three. Every tree you burn to survive tonight is one you won’t have next year.”

One tree, three meanings

Summer

Shade

The only shelter from the sun, besides the porch.

Fall

Food

Fruit, seeds for next spring, and branches.

Winter

Fire

Chop it for 2 or 3 logs, and it’s gone forever.

You can’t have all three. Every tree you burn tonight is one you won’t have next year.

This is the one idea the whole game is built on. Say it slowly, then press Enter in the game to start spring.

Each season changes the rules

Spring

Seeds can only be planted now.

Danger: rabbits eat sprouts.

Summer

Water sprouts from a shrinking pond.

Danger: heat outside the shade.

Fall

Sprouts become trees. Gather fruit and wood in the wind.

Danger: fruit left out rots.

Winter

Dark and slow. Keep the fire burning.

Danger: cold and wolves.

Play through the year while this is up. Spring: plant two seeds, chase off a rabbit. “Seeds can only go in the ground in spring. The wheel in the corner is a clock you plan around.” Summer: step out of the shade and show health draining; step under a tree. Fill the bucket and water a sprout: “If it dries out, it won’t be a tree in fall.” Fall: fruit falls, wind pushes you. “Your basket holds six. Whatever you leave on the ground rots when winter comes.” Winter: feed the fire, grab glowing deadwood in the dark, run back to the light when a wolf howls.

The first year teaches without text

  • Keycaps under the character until you move
  • Picture bubbles point to the next useful thing
  • Each season opens with two picture rules
  • The pause screen shows every season in pictures
Don’t explain anything here. Point at the game teaching itself: keycaps under the character, then a seed bubble, then a plot bubble. A crossed-out sprout shows seeds can’t be planted out of season.

The winter choice

Three logs now, or shade and fruit next year?

Each tree is carved with the year you planted it. Chop it and the stump shows its rings, while a ghost of its summer shade flashes over it.

You see exactly what you gave up.

Chop a tree live. “This is the choice the whole game is built on. Three logs now, or shade and fruit next year.” Point at the ghost of the tree and its rings. Each new year is harder too: hotter summers, colder and hungrier winters, more rabbits and wolves.

The sun jar: summer, saved for winter

Summer

Fill it by standing in the sun. The heat costs you health.

Winter

Open it in the dark for 10 seconds of warmth, light and full speed.

It doesn’t stop wolves. That’s the dog’s job.

Walk away from the fire and open the jar you filled in summer. The dark turns gold around you. “This is summer in a jar. You paid for it with health in the heat, and now you can spend it on a trip into the dark.” The season itself becomes a resource you store and spend.

Animals that remember you

The stray dog

Adopt it in summer. Its house costs 4 wood, the same wood your fire needs. Then it chases rabbits, fetches fruit and barks wolves away.

Rabbit and crow

Feed a hungry rabbit in fall and it guards your sprouts next spring. Let the crow take a seed and it returns two.

The porch cat

Arrives in year 2 and naps on the porch beam. Pet it and it purrs.

In summer, walk up to the stray dog and press Space to adopt it. “It costs 4 wood for its house, and that’s the same wood you need for the fire.” In winter, if the dog has a house, let it bark a wolf away. Without a house it shivers by the fire. Mention the rabbit and crow as choices that pay back next year, and the cat as a small reward for lasting into year 2.

Season changes are gameplay

Transitions you play through

  • Surviving sprouts become trees
  • Fruit left on the ground rots
  • Dusk falls at the end of fall
  • The ice cracks, and drops you in at the thaw

One weather event, warned on the wheel

Summer wildfire, fall gust, winter blizzard.

If there’s time, stand on the ice at the end of winter so the cracks show, then step off. The red dot on the season wheel shows when the weather event will hit, and a red warning counts down 3 seconds before it. Wildfire: at most one tree and two sprouts catch; one splash from the bucket puts each out. Gust: double wind, fruit flies across the map and sinks if it lands in the pond. Blizzard: the light shrinks and a wolf comes in.

Made during the jam, playable anywhere

Art and audio

Every pixel drawn in code, in one paper diorama style with four palettes.

Music and sound synthesized live, a different key and instrument each season.

Accessibility

Remappable keys, colour-blind palette, three difficulties, hint levels, reduce motion and volume.

Runs anywhere

One HTML file, offline, no install. Keyboard, gamepad or touch.

Zero third-party assets: no engine, library, image, sound or font file.

Open Options in the game and show the remappable keys, the colour-blind palette, the three difficulties, hint levels, reduce motion and volume. Mention it was playtested in headless Chromium with zero console errors, and that a scripted bot played full games at every difficulty to tune balance.

How Perennial meets the brief

CriterionWhat we built
ThemeThe seasons are the rules, not a backdrop.
Gameplay and funA short year, a tense first winter, a “one more year” pull.
Art and audioOne style, four palettes, live music for each season.
Playable buildOne offline file. Survive 3 winters to win, then endless.
OriginalityOne object changes meaning every season, and the season itself is stored in a jar.
“Take the seasons away and there is no game. Planting, watering, harvesting and burning only make sense in that order, on a clock.” Most season games swap tilesets; here the tree changes meaning every season, and the strongest move in winter is the weakest move for next year.

Perennial means something that comes back every year.

So do the players.
